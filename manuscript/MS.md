# **Abstract**

**Background**: Over 2023 and 2024, 19 countries supported by Gavi to purchase HPV vaccines adopted a single-dose HPV vaccination schedule. The goal of this study is to estimate the impact on vaccination access and the number of cervical cancers averted compared to use under a two-dose schedule.

**Methods**: We estimated the population that could be targeted in countries supported by Gavi to purchase HPV vaccines. We used UNICEF shipment plans to identify the number of HPV doses shipped to each country in 2023 and 2024, plus information supplied by Gavi on the dose schedule implemented in each country and year, adjusting for vaccine wastage. We computed the number of girls that could have been reached, first assuming complete utilization of all shipped doses under a single-dose schedule, and second assuming a counterfactual scenario where all countries would have used a 2-dose schedule. We then compared this to country-reported data on the number of girls actually vaccinated. For each of the three scenarios we modeled the number of cervical cancers averted using HPVsim, a microsimulation model calibrated to multiple countries.

**Findings**: We calculate that the introduction of single-dose HPV vaccination in Gavi-supported countries would have allowed these countries to target 23.3M additional girls if all supply was utilized. Reported data on girls vaccinated indicates that in actuality an additional 18.5M girls were reached due to adoption of single-dose. We estimate that the use of single-dose schedule in 2023 and 2024 could have averted up to 370,000 (356,000–376,000) additional future cervical cancers if all supply had been utilized, and 300,000 (278,000–304,000) given actual utilization.

**Interpretation**: The single-dose HPV vaccination strategy has had a substantial positive impact on cervical cancer elimination in context of supply constraints affecting low and middle-income countries.

# **Research in Context**

**Evidence before this study**  
In April 2022, the World Health Organisation (WHO) endorsed single-dose HPV vaccination based on evidence showing comparable protection to multi-dose regimens. Modeling studies have estimated that reaching 90% vaccine coverage would avert more than 60M cervical cancer cases over 100 years. However, global coverage had plateaued at 17% over 2017–2021, with Gavi-supported countries below 10% until 2022\. We searched PubMed using ("HPV vaccine") AND ("single dose") AND ("Gavi" OR "low-income countries") AND ("coverage" OR "implementation"). No studies quantified how many additional girls were vaccinated or cancers averted due to single-dose adoption in real-world settings.

**Added value of this study**  
Using UNICEF shipment data and microsimulation models calibrated to 16 countries supported by Gavi to purchase HPV vaccines, we estimated the additional number of girls that were able to be vaccinated under single-dose regimens compared to two-dose schedules with equivalent supply, and translated this to an estimate of additional cervical cancer cases averted over the next century. Our analysis provides concrete evidence of how WHO's policy change translated into measurable public health impact.

**Implications of all the available evidence**  
All available evidence indicates that single-dose HPV vaccination offers a realistic and expedited pathway toward cervical cancer elimination targets, and that the introduction of single-dose in numerous Gavi-supported countries has already been operationally transformative, enabling substantially greater reach with limited supplies.

# **Introduction**

Vaccination against human papillomavirus (HPV), the causative agent of cervical cancer, has been slowly scaling up since its introduction in 2007\.[1](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?5Oh4xZ) When administered prior to sexual debut, HPV vaccines have been shown to provide durable and highly-effective protection against infection, pre-cancers and more recently cancers, caused by HPV.[2–7](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?bsoc5h) HPV vaccines were initially licensed as a three-dose regimen, but studies subsequently demonstrated that a single dose provides a comparable protection.[8–12](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?YjWn1d) The WHO’s Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization formally endorsed single-dose HPV vaccination in 2022\.[13](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?Do18Kf) Over the following 3 years, the number of countries that had introduced HPV vaccination programs jumped from 130 in 2022 to more than 150 in 2025, with over half of these following single-dose HPV vaccination regimens.[14](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?YC5NyG)

Prior to the single-dose recommendation, there was already a wealth of evidence demonstrating the benefits of scaled-up HPV vaccination. Mathematical model studies published in 2020 estimated that reaching 90% coverage of HPV vaccination among 78 lower- and middle-income countries (LMICs) could avert 61M cases of cervical cancer over a 100-year period.[15,16](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?d7piOj) Studies have also shown that HPV vaccination is cost-effective,[17](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?cI8lHn) safe,[4](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?BcqhdF) and is already associated with population-level declines in precancer and cancer incidence in countries where it has been operating for multiple years.[18–21](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?0i5wU0) Despite all this evidence, global HPV vaccine coverage levels had plateaued at \~17% over 2017–2021, before registering a 10 percentage point increase between 2022-2024 following the WHO’s single-dose regimen endorsement.[22](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?hXYUr1)

In an effort to help boost HPV vaccination rates among the world’s lowest-income countries, Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, added support for HPV vaccines in 2012\. However, supply constraints and implementation challenges led to HPV vaccine coverage below 10% in Gavi-supported countries up until 2022\. Coverage began to increase more rapidly following the WHO’s single-dose recommendation, which improved supply and increased investment in HPV vaccination. By the end of 2024, 42 countries out of the 81 eligible for Gavi HPV support had introduced HPV vaccination[23](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?oCQgll), of which 31 received and rolled out doses purchased with Gavi support between 2023 and 2024\. Of these 31 countries, 18 adopted a single-dose regimen by the end of 2024\. Across these 31 countries, there were more than 90,000 new cases of invasive cervical cancer in 2020 (\~15% of the global burden), as well as 60,000 cervical cancer deaths (\~20% of the global total). The HPV vaccines used in Gavi-supported countries during 2023-2024 were primarily nonavalent (protecting against HPV types 16, 18, 6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, and 58), with some countries receiving bivalent vaccines (protecting against types 16 and 18, which cause approximately 70% of cervical cancers globally).

The goal of this study is to determine how many additional girls were able to be vaccinated as a result of the adoption of a single-dose schedule in Gavi-supported countries in 2023 and 2024, compared to a counterfactual scenario where all countries would have used a 2-dose schedule. We also use a microsimulation model (HPVsim) to estimate how this increase in the number of vaccinated girls would translate to an eventual reduction in cervical cancer cases over the next century. 

# **Methods**

**Country scope**  
Countries supported by Gavi in 2023 and 2024 to procure HPV vaccines through UNICEF Supply Division were included in the analysis. The 31 countries that received HPV vaccines with purchasing support from Gavi between 2023-2024 and which had HPV vaccination programs over this period are: Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cambodia, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Gambia, Kenya, Kosovo, Kyrgyzstan, Lao PDR, Lesotho, Liberia, Malawi, Mali, Mauritania, Mozambique, Myanmar, Nigeria, Rwanda, Sao Tome & Principe, Senegal, Sierra Leone, Solomon Islands, Sri Lanka, Tanzania, Togo, Uganda, Zambia, and Zimbabwe. The combined population across these 31 countries is just over 1 billion people, with life expectancy at birth ranging from 53 (Lesotho and Nigeria) to 77 (Sri Lanka and Kosovo). Table S1 summarizes key features of the 31 countries. Of these 31 countries, 19 were using a single-dose HPV vaccination regimen by the end of 2024 (Table S1). In the following analyses, we calculate the total number of girls vaccinated across all 31 countries, and we estimate the impact of using a single-dose regimen on cervical cancer by focusing on the 19 countries that had introduced single-dose.

**Vaccine supply**  
For each country, we used UNICEF shipment plans to identify the number of HPV doses shipped to each country in 2023 and 2024\. We did not have data on pre-existing vaccine stocks at country level for January 2023\. However, given the supply constraints in preceding years and that most countries were either newly introducing HPV vaccination or had limited prior programs, we assumed minimal existing stocks at the start of 2023\. This means our analysis focuses on the impact of doses shipped during 2023-2024, which represents the vast majority of doses available to these programs during this period. We used Gavi information on the dose schedule implemented in each country and year and adjusted for vaccine wastage rate,  assumed as 5 or 10% depending on product as specified by Gavi HPV vaccine profiles.[24](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?ChqGgw) We calculated the number of girls that were actually reached in 2023 and 2024 using country-reported data to the WHO,1[4](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?Vu129P) and compared this both to the number that could have been reached assuming complete utilization of supply, and to a scenario where all programs would have used a 2-dose schedule and complete supply utilization (counterfactual). We treated the additional number of girls that could be reached as a net gain, as all doses available to Gavi countries were shipped in 2023 and 2024\. For countries that transitioned from a two-dose to a one-dose schedule during 2023 or 2024, girls who had received one dose under the previous two-dose schedule were considered fully vaccinated after the policy change, consistent with WHO guidance. 

**Modeling impact on cervical cancer**  
We modeled additional future cervical cancers averted between scenarios using HPVsim,[25,26](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?TvjIFj) a flexible software package built on the Starsim disease modeling platform.[27](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?Xp3DQr) HPVsim was specifically developed for creating agent-based models of HPV transmission and cervical disease progression, and has thus far been used for modeling screening scenarios in India and Tunisia,[25](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?foyYgZ) as well as models for multiple countries across Sub-Saharan Africa.[28](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?vsaYCx) For this analysis, we created HPVsim models for 16 of the 19 countries using a single-dose regimen (we did not create models for Solomon Islands or Kosovo due to a lack of data). Countries that switched to a single-dose schedule during the year were analyzed as single-dose programmes if the switch occurred in the first half of the year, or before the majority of HPV vaccination outreaches were conducted in that country. Together, these 16 countries were allocated 99.9% of the total number of doses distributed to countries where single-dose was introduced. Each of the 16 country models was populated with context-specific demographic, behavioral, and programmatic data, and then calibrated to match cervical cancer data published by GLOBOCAN using a hyperparameter optimization algorithm.[28](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?GxSn7H) More details on the models, including the fit to data, are presented in the Supplementary Materials.

To estimate the impact of the single-dose vaccination strategy, we ran four scenarios for each country: an ‘actual utilization’ scenario in which we capture actual number of girls vaccinated  and delivery schedules in each country, an ‘complete utilization’ scenario assuming that doses were allocated according to the actual delivery schedule but with 100% utilization (after accounting for wastage), a ‘counterfactual 2-dose’ scenario in which doses were delivered as a two-dose schedule, and a ‘no vaccination’ scenario where no doses were allocated. For each scenario, we calculated the number of cervical cancer cases occurring among the cohort eligible for vaccination.

We assumed the same protection and durability of protection between dose schedules based on WHO's 2022 recommendation, which was grounded in evidence showing non-inferior protection from single-dose compared to multi-dose regimens[13](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?S3MSCc). Specifically, we assumed that one dose provides equivalent long-term protection against HPV 16/18 infections as two doses, with no waning of protection over the time horizon modeled. For vaccines protecting against additional types (nonavalent), we assumed equivalent cross-protection patterns. 

To isolate the impact of the doses delivered over 2023 and 2024, we do not consider ongoing vaccination programs before or after these two years, vaccine stocks, or screening and treatment programs for cervical cancer. We ran 20 replicates for each scenario using different random number seeds, and calculated the median and 10-90% range of the number of averted cervical cancer cases. These four scenarios are summarized in Table 1\. 

| Scenario | Dose Schedule | Utilization | Purpose |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | ----- |
| **No vaccination** | N/A | N/A | Baseline for calculating impact |
| **Counterfactual 2-dose** | 2-dose for all countries | 100% of shipped doses (after wastage) | Counterfactual showing impact if single-dose had not been adopted |
| **Complete utilization** | Actual (1-dose or 2-dose per country) | 100% of shipped doses (after wastage) | Maximum potential impact of single-dose adoption |
| **Actual utilization** | Actual (1-dose or 2-dose per country) | Country-reported coverage | Observed real-world impact of single-dose adoption |

# **Results**

**Vaccine shipments and dose schedules**  
In 2023, 26 countries – with a combined total population of 12.0M 9 year old girls – received HPV vaccine doses purchased with Gavi support, of which 10 used a single-dose schedule. This increased in 2024 to 31 countries (with 13.0M 9 year old girls) receiving doses, of which 19 used a single-dose schedule. In total, 51.4M doses were allocated to single-dose programs, and 10.8M doses supported two-dose schedules in 2023 and 2024\. In Figure 1, we compare the number of doses allocated and girls reached in 2023 and 2024 under all three vaccination scenarios: complete utilization of the shipped doses, actual utilization  of the shipped doses, and the counterfactual in which all countries used two-dose schedules. Adopting a single-dose vaccination regimen would have enabled countries to target 23.3M additional girls if all doses were utilized, compared to administering the equivalent vaccine supply through two-dose programs.

**Vaccine coverage estimates**  
For the remainder of the results, we focus on the 16 countries that used single-dose HPV regimens in 2023 and/or 2024 and for which we created simulation models. In total across these 16 countries, there were 58M girls aged 9-14 in 2024\. Under the counterfactual scenario where HPV vaccination was delivered as a 2-dose regimen, the programs could have vaccinated at most 24.2M girls (equivalent to 42% of this cohort). Thanks to the introduction of single-dose regimens, we estimate that the number of girls reached in 2024 could have increased to 47.5M (81% of the 9-14 cohort) assuming complete utilization, or to 42.7M (73%) assuming actual utilization. Actual reported vaccination coverage for each country varies depending on each country’s target vaccination cohort.

**Cervical cancer impact**  
Without any HPV vaccination or screening/treatment programs, we estimate there would be just over 1.62M (range: 1.55–1.69M) cases of cervical cancer over 2025–2125 in the cohort of girls eligible for vaccination in 2023/24 across the 16 countries (Figure 2A). In the counterfactual scenario in which HPV vaccination consists of a 2-dose regimen, we estimate 1.22M (range: 1.17-1.28M) cancers in this same cohort; thus, even without the shift to single-dose administration, HPV vaccination would avert 394k cases of cervical cancer. Compared to this counterfactual, we estimate that the use of single-dose schedule in 2023 and 2024 averted up to 297,000 (range: 222,000-369,000) additional future cervical cancers (Figure 2B), resulting in a total of 928k cases of cervical cancer over the next 100 years among all girls eligible for vaccination in 2023/24 across all 16 modeled countries (Figure 2A). Had all doses been utilized, this would have averted a further 72k cancers among the same cohort, or 370k cancers averted in total compared to the 2-dose counterfactual.   
![Figure 2](../figures/fig2_ts.png)
*Figure 2: Estimates over 2025–2125 of the cumulative number of cervical cancers cases (panel A) and cases averted due to single-dose introduction (panel B).*

Of the 370k cervical cancer cases that could have been averted assuming complete utilization and a single-dose schedule compared to the 2-dose counterfactual, we estimate that over 217k cases (59% of the total averted) were attributable to countries that introduced vaccination as a single-dose regimen in 2023, 5k (1%) were from countries that first introduced HPV vaccination in 2024 using a single-dose regimen, and the remaining 148k (40%) were from countries that switched from a 2-dose to a 1-dose regimen in 2024\. By country, the number of cervical cancer cases that could have been averted due the use of single-dose vaccination approximately tracks the number of additional girls that could have been vaccinated in each country, with more than 17M girls vaccinated and 262k averted cancers across Ethiopia, Nigeria, and Bangladesh alone. Dividing the total number of additional girls vaccinated due to the introduction of single-dose (23.3M) by the number of cervical cancer cases averted by this shift (370k), we arrive at an estimated number needed to vaccinate (NNV) to prevent one cervical cancer case of 63, taken across all 16 countries. 

# **Discussion**

In 2023 and 2024, the adoption of single-dose HPV vaccination regimens increased significantly, resulting in the vaccination of \~18.5 million additional girls across 31 countries supported by Gavi to purchase HPV vaccines compared to a two-dose schedule with complete utilization of available doses. Focusing on the 16 countries that had adopted single-dose regimens by 2024, we estimated that this would represent an increase from 42% to 73% of all girls aged 9-14 in 2024 fully vaccinated, averting approximately 297,000 additional cervical cancer cases over the next century. We estimate that among the modeled countries, one case of cervical cancer can be prevented with every additional 63 girls vaccinated, though effectiveness varies by country due to differences in HPV genotype prevalence, cancer rates, and population demographics. Our estimates of the number needed to vaccinate (NNV) to prevent one case of cervical cancer are broadly in line with previous estimates (of 78 to 381 among four LMICs).[29](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?g6utx4)

This result highlights the significant impact of the single-dose recommendation and action by Ministries of health to implement single-dose schedules on cervical cancer outcomes. UNICEF shipped all available HPV vaccine doses to Gavi-supported countries in 2023 and 2024, thus the population that could be targeted over and above the 2-dose counterfactual scenario represents a net gain \- those girls could not have been targeted in the absence of a 1-dose recommendation due to insufficient vaccine availability.[30](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?RLFjIy) The 1-dose recommendation may offer additional benefits over and above the number of girls that could be reached, including resource sparing due to reduced service delivery requirements, potentially lower vaccine and vial purchasing costs (though we did not conduct a formal economic evaluation in this analysis), reduced cold chain capacity requirements and a reduced burden on targeted girls.

Our findings support the WHO recommendation for single-dose HPV vaccination in the primary target age group (9-14 years), particularly in settings facing supply constraints. However, single-dose schedules are most effective when vaccination occurs before sexual debut, making age at vaccination a critical consideration. For older adolescents and young adults who may have already been exposed to HPV, and for immunocompromised individuals including those living with HIV, WHO currently recommends multi-dose schedules based on available evidence. For countries introducing HPV vaccination for the first time, single-dose schedules offer operational advantages beyond supply efficiency, including simplified logistics, reduced cold chain requirements, and decreased burden on targeted populations. As global vaccine supply improves, decisions about dose schedules should balance efficiency, evidence on long-term protection, and equity considerations to ensure maximum population benefit.

Our analysis has several limitations. We assumed that all doses would be allocated to girls in each country’s target cohort; in reality, actual delivery may have reached girls from different age groups and/or boys in some cases. We assumed no pre-existing vaccine stocks as of January 2023, but if substantial stocks existed in some countries, this could affect the precision of our estimates for individual countries, though it would not change the overall conclusion about the impact of single-dose adoption. In order to isolate the impact of single-dose regimen adoption in 2023 and 2024, we did not include any other vaccination, screening, or treatment programs in our modeling. Therefore the estimates of future cervical cancers that are presented throughout the analysis are not projections of the likely true number of future cases given the continuation of ongoing cervical screening and vaccination programs (noting, however, that screening coverage remains low in most settings, ranging between 3-20%[31](https://www.zotero.org/google-docs/?CvE0Xh)). In addition, we did not include the indirect (herd) effects of vaccination on transmission to unvaccinated cohorts, whereby vaccination prevents onward viral transmission that could infect other age groups. Within the vaccinated cohorts, transmission dynamics between vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals are captured by the model. However, we excluded herd effects to other cohorts because we were evaluating the impact of two years of vaccination in isolation, and estimates of the broader herd benefits would have been unrealistically high if other ongoing vaccination and screening programs were not included. Our estimates of the impact on cervical cancer are derived from modeling results, which are subject to the usual model limitations, including parameter uncertainty and stochasticity. In particular, uncertainty around how the allocated doses of HPV vaccine were actually distributed among girls of different ages is likely to influence the results, particularly for countries implementing multi-age cohorts to vaccinate 9-14 year olds, as an important determinant of HPV vaccine effectiveness is whether the vaccine is delivered before or after the  age of sexual debut. To the extent possible, we accounted for these factors by running multiple random seeds and averaging the results, and by calibrating our models to available data to minimize parameter uncertainty.  
A key assumption underlying our analysis is that the additional girls vaccinated under single-dose schedules represent genuine expansion of program reach, not merely a vaccination that would have occurred anyway under two-dose schedules with proportionally increased supply. This assumption is well-supported by the global HPV vaccine supply context during 2023-2024. According to Gavi's Market Shaping Roadmap, manufacturers were operating at maximum production capacity during this period, with demand substantially outstripping available supply.30 The Gavi Alliance operated a supply allocation framework based on a set of criteria including country readiness, applications status and available supply: countries were allocated supply based on the date of approval of their application and existing supply capacity. If countries had not utilized a single-dose schedule but remained on a 2-dose schedule, fewer countries would have been able to introduce, as all available supply was allocated. The Gavi Alliance left countries to choose their dosing schedule, with no country was penalized for a 2-dose schedule. The WHO's endorsement of single-dose schedules thus provided a critical pathway for countries to maximize the public health impact of fundamentally constrained supply. The transition to single-dose schedules enabled scale-up with existing manufacturing capacity, eliminating the need to wait for production expansion.

HPV vaccination is well established as a highly effective strategy for significantly reducing the global burden of cervical cancer. The widespread adoption of single-dose regimens has demonstrated the potential to expand reach, improve coverage rates, and prevent hundreds of thousands of future cancer cases. As countries continue to implement and scale up HPV vaccination programs, they contribute not only to individual health benefits but also to broader public health goals of equity and disease eradication. Prioritizing single-dose administration of the HPV vaccine allows more girls to be reached, and thus more cancer prevented, bringing us closer to the ultimate goal of cervical cancer elimination.
